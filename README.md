# WIPlife

A free, mobile-first life simulator that runs entirely on the player's device. Every life is a work in progress.

- Game design: [docs/design.md](docs/design.md)
- Architecture, data model and roadmap: [docs/technical.md](docs/technical.md)
- Rules for coding AIs: [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md)
- Playable game: https://wiplife.pages.dev (hosted on Cloudflare Pages)

First-time Playwright setup: `npx playwright install chromium`.

Development and test builds have an event sandbox at `/?sandbox`: it previews any event with any pronoun set and character state. Production builds leave it out.

App icons in `public/` are generated from `public/favicon.svg` with `npx pwa-assets-generator`.

`npm run simulate` also reports the mean milliseconds per simulated year (the whole headless year, invariant checks included) and fails when the careful or careless player exceeds `performance.maxMsPerYear` in `balance/targets.yaml`, so a stage that makes the game slower is caught in its own pull request.

Language skills (W4b) appear on Profile, where adults can take one paid course per year. School classes, living locally and household use also build skills; unused languages fade slowly. `balance/languages.yaml` controls the rates, social effects and local job requirements. The simulation's Languages section reports native and learned skills, learning/fading, language-blocked job opportunities and friendship observations.

W5 adds More → Plan a trip and Work/School → study or temporary work abroad. Its simulation section reports travel, destinations, encounters, temporary stays, return rates, immersion and distance effects. Countries outside the four full country models are vacation-only destinations.

W6 adds More → Move abroad and citizenship. Four legal routes (work sponsorship, study then work, family and marriage, investment) have eligibility, costs, waits and odds per country in `balance/immigration.yaml`; temporary status can become permanent residence and then citizenship (with a test), and dual citizenship works. Without papers a life has informal work with lower pay, no loans or licenses, a yearly risk of removal linked to the legal system, stress, and uncertain routes to status. Money can be sent home through the yearly ledger. Citizenship of children follows birthplace and parentage, and people you know can emigrate. The simulation's Emigration and citizenship section reports moves by route and country, time to citizenship, undocumented outcomes and remittances.

B1 (playtest fixes) changes three things. Romance: you can flirt with, go on a date with and ask out any unrelated adult you are attracted to, whatever their orientation; someone who isn't attracted to you says a kind, clear no, and partners still need attraction both ways. People → Find a date (18+, single) looks for someone new by a dating app, a friend's set-up or a night out, with less luck each try in a year. Custom creation now asks you to choose who you are attracted to (No one is a choice). Names: people and your children carry a name heritage (from a parent, or from the city they live in: `heritageWeights` on a city), and every heritage has at least 50 first names per gender category and 50 last names (the content build checks it). Sports: only games, playoff and big-moment events and playing through pain cause injuries; training never does. The simulation's "Romance by attraction" section compares partnering and years single by who a life is attracted to (`balance/targets.yaml attraction`).

Batch 2a adds More → Activities: leisure and self, grouped by category (Mind & body, Salon & spa, Plastic surgery, Shopping, Movie theater, Nightlife, Outdoor, Luxury, Love). Activities are content in `src/content/pastimes` with every number in `balance/pastimes.yaml`; one life action, `do_activity`, runs any of them through finance, health, the law and relationships. Luxury buys valuables (a new possession kind). `npm run simulate` has an Activities section (per-category use, spending, surgery complications, nightlife outcomes, valuables, diminishing returns under the spamming player) with its targets.

Batch 2b adds six more groups to More → Activities: Crime, Black market, Gambling, Lawsuits, Identity and Loans. They are the same activity type and the same `do_activity` action as Batch 2a, with `mechanic`s of their own (`gamble`, `lawsuit`, `loan`, `name`) and every number in `balance/risk.yaml` (the rates and terms of the new `bank`, `payday` and `shark` debt kinds are in `balance/economy.yaml`). Consequences run through the legal, finance, health and vice systems and E6a's heat and dirty money: no second record, debt or addiction exists. `npm run simulate` has a "Risk and legal activities" section (crime, the black market, gambling returns against each game's table, lawsuits, name changes, loans and the interest the ledger charged) with its targets; `--player criminal` (also in `--player all`) is the one who commits crimes, deals and borrows from sharks, and the careful player never does.

Batch 3 makes school and work feel like the country and the people in them. School follows the country: the US names high school years freshman to senior and holds the SAT or ACT; Canada's provincial exams count toward the final mark, and Montreal's schools end a year earlier with a CEGEP before university; the UK has GCSEs at 16 with a choice (stay on for the sixth form, a further education college, an apprenticeship or work) and A-levels at 18 that confirm a university place; Mexico's secundaria ends with a placement exam for preparatoria and a university entrance exam follows. The School card on the Work/School tab has a yearly report card (4 to 6 subjects, in words), this year's actions (study harder, slack off, a tutor, cheat on a test, skip class), your class and teachers (each opens the interaction menu, with ask for help, suck up, argue and report a problem for teachers), and clubs and teams for younger children. The Work tab's job card lists your boss and coworkers (how many depends on the size of the workplace) and the work actions. Numbers are in `src/content/balance/school.yaml` (and `careers.yaml` workplace and workActions); the countries' exams and choice are in `src/content/countries/*.yaml` (`school.exams`, `school.choice`, `school.curriculum`) and a city's changes in `education:` on the city (Montreal). Teachers are never romance and nothing romantic involves anyone under 18 (the engine enforces both and the content build checks them).

Batch 4 adds music's grind to the Fame screen (Work → Fame): bands (form one by holding auditions, join one that asks, leave or go solo; as the leader rename it, ask a member to leave and recruit a replacement), your sound (17 genres in `src/content/genres`, affinities in `balance/fame.yaml sound`), shows (up to 6 a year, by rung) and a tour (one a year, sized by rung; it replaces the tour box on projects), and practice (a yearly session budget, any path can register skills in `src/content/fame`). The project step takes a genre and a title you type (track titles optional). Numbers are in `balance/fame.yaml` (`practice`, `shows`, `tours`, `band`, `sound`). `npm run simulate -- --player star` reports a music grind line (shows and tours a career year, practice, bands formed, joined and split, member changes, genre switches, off-genre releases), how far music careers begun as a teen get by 28 with and without a fitting talent, and how often careers without a fitting talent pass the middle by path, with targets in `balance/targets.yaml fame`.
