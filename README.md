# WIPlife

A free, mobile-first life simulator that runs entirely on the player's device. Every life is a work in progress.

- Game design: [docs/design.md](docs/design.md)
- Architecture, data model and roadmap: [docs/technical.md](docs/technical.md)
- Rules for coding AIs: [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md)
- Playable game: https://wiplife.pages.dev (hosted on Cloudflare Pages)

First-time Playwright setup: `npx playwright install chromium`.

Development and test builds have an event sandbox at `/?sandbox`: it previews any event with any pronoun set and character state. Production builds leave it out.

App icons in `public/` are generated from `public/favicon.svg` with `npx pwa-assets-generator`.

`npm run simulate` also reports the mean milliseconds per simulated year (the whole headless year, invariant checks included) and fails when the careful or careless player exceeds `performance.maxMsPerYear` in `balance/targets.yaml`, so a stage that makes the game slower is caught in its own pull request.

Language skills (W4b) appear on Profile, where adults can take one paid course per year. School classes, living locally and household use also build skills; unused languages fade slowly. `balance/languages.yaml` controls the rates, social effects and local job requirements. The simulation's Languages section reports native and learned skills, learning/fading, language-blocked job opportunities and friendship observations.

W5 adds More → Plan a trip and Work/School → study or temporary work abroad. Its simulation section reports travel, destinations, encounters, temporary stays, return rates, immersion and distance effects. Countries outside the four full country models are vacation-only destinations.

W6 adds More → Move abroad and citizenship. Four legal routes (work sponsorship, study then work, family and marriage, investment) have eligibility, costs, waits and odds per country in `balance/immigration.yaml`; temporary status can become permanent residence and then citizenship (with a test), and dual citizenship works. Without papers a life has informal work with lower pay, no loans or licenses, a yearly risk of removal linked to the legal system, stress, and uncertain routes to status. Money can be sent home through the yearly ledger. Citizenship of children follows birthplace and parentage, and people you know can emigrate. The simulation's Emigration and citizenship section reports moves by route and country, time to citizenship, undocumented outcomes and remittances.

B1 (playtest fixes) changes three things. Romance: you can flirt with, go on a date with and ask out any unrelated adult you are attracted to, whatever their orientation; someone who isn't attracted to you says a kind, clear no, and partners still need attraction both ways. People → Find a date (18+, single) looks for someone new by a dating app, a friend's set-up or a night out, with less luck each try in a year. Custom creation now asks you to choose who you are attracted to (No one is a choice). Names: people and your children carry a name heritage (from a parent, or from the city they live in: `heritageWeights` on a city), and every heritage has at least 50 first names per gender category and 50 last names (the content build checks it). Sports: only games, playoff and big-moment events and playing through pain cause injuries; training never does. The simulation's "Romance by attraction" section compares partnering and years single by who a life is attracted to (`balance/targets.yaml attraction`).

Batch 2a adds More → Activities: leisure and self, grouped by category (Mind & body, Salon & spa, Plastic surgery, Shopping, Movie theater, Nightlife, Outdoor, Luxury, Love). Activities are content in `src/content/pastimes` with every number in `balance/pastimes.yaml`; one life action, `do_activity`, runs any of them through finance, health, the law and relationships. Luxury buys valuables (a new possession kind). `npm run simulate` has an Activities section (per-category use, spending, surgery complications, nightlife outcomes, valuables, diminishing returns under the spamming player) with its targets.

Batch 2b adds six more groups to More → Activities: Crime, Black market, Gambling, Lawsuits, Identity and Loans. They are the same activity type and the same `do_activity` action as Batch 2a, with `mechanic`s of their own (`gamble`, `lawsuit`, `loan`, `name`) and every number in `balance/risk.yaml` (the rates and terms of the new `bank`, `payday` and `shark` debt kinds are in `balance/economy.yaml`). Consequences run through the legal, finance, health and vice systems and E6a's heat and dirty money: no second record, debt or addiction exists. `npm run simulate` has a "Risk and legal activities" section (crime, the black market, gambling returns against each game's table, lawsuits, name changes, loans and the interest the ledger charged) with its targets; `--player criminal` (also in `--player all`) is the one who commits crimes, deals and borrows from sharks, and the careful player never does.

Batch 3 makes school and work feel like the country and the people in them. School follows the country: the US names high school years freshman to senior and holds the SAT or ACT; Canada's provincial exams count toward the final mark, and Montreal's schools end a year earlier with a CEGEP before university; the UK has GCSEs at 16 with a choice (stay on for the sixth form, a further education college, an apprenticeship or work) and A-levels at 18 that confirm a university place; Mexico's secundaria ends with a placement exam for preparatoria and a university entrance exam follows. The School card on the Work/School tab has a yearly report card (4 to 6 subjects, in words), this year's actions (study harder, slack off, a tutor, cheat on a test, skip class), your class and teachers (each opens the interaction menu, with ask for help, suck up, argue and report a problem for teachers), and clubs and teams for younger children. The Work tab's job card lists your boss and coworkers (how many depends on the size of the workplace) and the work actions. Numbers are in `src/content/balance/school.yaml` (and `careers.yaml` workplace and workActions); the countries' exams and choice are in `src/content/countries/*.yaml` (`school.exams`, `school.choice`, `school.curriculum`) and a city's changes in `education:` on the city (Montreal). Teachers are never romance and nothing romantic involves anyone under 18 (the engine enforces both and the content build checks them).

Batch 4 adds music's grind to the Fame screen (Work → Fame): bands (form one by holding auditions, join one that asks, leave or go solo; as the leader rename it, ask a member to leave and recruit a replacement), your sound (17 genres in `src/content/genres`, affinities in `balance/fame.yaml sound`), shows (up to 6 a year, by rung) and a tour (one a year, sized by rung; it replaces the tour box on projects), and practice (a yearly session budget, any path can register skills in `src/content/fame`). The project step takes a genre and a title you type (track titles optional). Numbers are in `balance/fame.yaml` (`practice`, `shows`, `tours`, `band`, `sound`). `npm run simulate -- --player star` reports a music grind line (shows and tours a career year, practice, bands formed, joined and split, member changes, genre switches, off-genre releases), how far music careers begun as a teen get by 28 with and without a fitting talent, and how often careers without a fitting talent pass the middle by path, with targets in `balance/targets.yaml fame`.

Batch 5 turns sports into a grind (Work → Sports). You choose which team to try out for at every level: youth rec, club, travel and (in the UK and Mexico) academy teams, school junior varsity and varsity, college programs, and pro camps. Each team has a bar, you see your odds in words, and the scrimmage is an event whose choice is how you play it; a cut can be caught by the next team down, or end with no team. A school team in a sport is the sports career's school rung and one record with your teams and clubs. Training sessions are an action with a yearly budget (more with a stronger commitment) that raises the sport's skills (`skills` in `src/content/fame/*.yaml`, Batch 4's practice system): with your team's coaches at no charge, or without a team at a gym (a yearly membership) or with a private coach (paid per session), both at a lower gain; training never injures. Athletic scholarships come from college programs by rating, fame, grades and position need, take a share of tuition off the bill through the education system, keep a grade floor and end when you lose eligibility or leave the team; whether and how much a country pays is `athletics` in `src/content/countries/*.yaml`. The draft card says in words where scouts project you, combine events move it, rookie pay is a curve of the pick plus skill incentives and a signing bonus, and a player the draft passed by (or a pro without a deal) can go to camps and open tryouts every year until an age limit. Players also get season choices: role battles, asking for minutes, the offseason, a jersey number and the captain's band. Numbers are in `balance/sports.yaml` (`tryout`, `training`, `scholarship`, `role`, `draft`, `contract`). `npm run simulate -- --player athlete` reports tryouts by level, scholarships by sport and country, the pick against rating and rookie pay by pick, undrafted players who later signed and training sessions with and without a team.

Batch 6 widens the sports world (Work → Sports). Pro teams now exist in more places: at least 10 US cities and a few Canadian ones that the game does not model yet (`futureCity` teams, each with a `cityName`, the `nearestCityId` you live in when you sign, and home games you travel to), and four countries it does not model (Spain, Italy, Germany and France, `futureCountry` soccer clubs with fictional names in real cities; a player in the UK signs with one, lives in a UK city and commutes, with teammates from that country's name pool). `npm run content` prints the list of `futureCity` teams. A sport's market (`leagues.pro.markets`) decides which other countries' clubs you can sign with: the US and Canada share a market in the four North American sports, and soccer's market is every country with clubs; a club in another modeled country moves you there on a work permit that runs past the deal (W6's permit record). Soccer has youth academies tied to a pro club (from 8 to 16; a UK child can have a trial at an academy abroad and commute), promotion of an academy graduate to the first team at 18, loans (the club that holds your deal decides), a yearly transfer window (offers by rating, age and the years left on the deal, fees between the clubs with a share to you, release clauses, an agent who negotiates) and two leagues in every country with promotion and relegation. Every sport has a national side with call-ups and a fictional international tournament, and clubs with a rival (the same city or region) have derbies. Soccer is "football" where the country's words say so (`athletics.soccerWord` in `src/content/countries`; the other football is "American football" there). Settings → Teams renames any team (name and nickname), moves it to another of the game's cities and adds teams of your own (name, nickname, city, a strength from 1 to 5); input is validated, stored in the settings database (schema version 2, with a migration) and copied into every new life as its own snapshot, so a life replays exactly whatever the settings say later. During a life, renames show at once (display only, keyed by team id); moved, added and removed teams reach it next season through the recorded `sync_teams` action. Numbers are in `balance/sports.yaml` (`world`, `soccer`, `international`); the clubs are in `src/content/fame/*.yaml`; the name pools are `src/content/names/{es,it,de,fr}.yaml`. `npm run simulate -- --player athlete` reports pro seasons by country and league, academies, loans, transfers with the fees and the players' share, commuting players, moves abroad (none by anyone under 18) and call-ups, with targets in `balance/targets.yaml sports`.

B2 (Batch 7, business depth) turns a business from a set of dials into a shop you run (Work → Business, now with Report, Products, Team, Places and Franchise tabs). Each kind sells product lines in content (`src/content/businesses/*.yaml`: `products`, `pools`, `roles`): a café's coffee, tea, cold drinks, pastries and sandwiches; a bakery's bread, rolls, cakes, pastries and custom orders; a shop that picks its own lines within a limit; services (salon, plumbing, cleaning) that draw on supply pools instead of shelves. Every line has its own price level and every line (or pool) its own order level (lean, normal, heavy against a forecast of last year's demand); stock is worked out once a year, perishables spoil, durables carry over, running out loses sales and a little reputation, and stock bought and not sold is inventory (counted at cost), not an expense. The world is a pure function of the life's seed and the year (`src/engine/business/world.ts`): an economy (boom, steady, slowdown, recession) that moves demand by each line's sensitivity and what banks lend, a price index for each supplier category (with shortages and better deals as events) and a demand index for each trend; the city's `competition` value finally takes trade from the lines that are easy to get elsewhere. Headcount is chosen per location and role (the staffing level is a preset that fills it); a few people (leads, the licensed, people you know, a location manager) are real people, the rest a counted crew per place and role with morale, turnover (one roll a pool) and training; you hire to a place and a role, move a person between places (across cities only if they are willing and have no family there), appoint a location manager, and a business that has earned it can franchise (fee, royalty, support, brand risk). Limits are 5 businesses, 24 locations each and 36 in all, held back by attention, money and managers. Numbers are in `balance/business.yaml` (`products`, `competition`, `economy`, `suppliers`, `trends`, `headcount`, `crew`, `training`, `locationManagers`, `franchise`, `limits`, `owner.attention`, `owner.span`); the supplier categories and trend groups are in `registries/business.yaml`; 45 events (all follow-ups the business step queues) are in `src/content/events/any/business`. `npm run simulate` has a "Business depth (B2)" block (stockouts, waste, margins by kind and product group, people a place and by size, location managers, turnover, profit by economy phase, supplier-shock years, survival by ordering policy and by the economy in the founding year, franchises, businesses an owner, milliseconds a year with and without a business) with its targets in `balance/targets.yaml business`.

Batch 8 adds social media on real platforms (More → Social media for everyone aged 13 and over; Work → Social media for creators). Six platforms are content in `src/content/platforms` (Instagram, TikTok, X / Twitter, YouTube, Twitch and Podcasts, the only real names in the game; no logos, no partnership or endorsement, no real people), each with its own growth model and its own way of paying (a share of ad money, subscriptions and donations, a creator fund, ad reads, a premium share, brand deals), and every number is in `balance/social.yaml`. You open an account on a platform with a handle you type (checked as you type) and a niche (`src/content/niches`), post inside a yearly budget with diminishing returns (a safe post or a bold one), keep accounts private, send the followers of one account to another, make something with creators you know and promote your music, film or season to your followers. Platforms pay through the yearly ledger once an account passes its threshold and keeps posting (the Money tab shows it by platform); a sponsorship is an E6b contract with a made-up brand (a parent signs under 18). The social media career's rung and fame follow your reach; a viral post is a big break; algorithm changes, demonetization, burnout, rows and cancellation use the gossip web (`onlinePost`) and the tabloids. Anyone can have a casual account, and a post you did not mean for them can reach your boss, partner, an ex or family. Under 18 an account needs a parent, starts private and a parent signs deals; nothing a minor can meet is romantic or suggestive (`tools/content/social.ts` checks it). `npm run simulate` has a Social media section for every player (the star player runs accounts as a creator) with targets in `balance/targets.yaml social`.


Batch 10 (language and travel, expanded) adds more ways to learn a language and more to do when you travel. Profile → Languages now offers a class, an app (cheap, slow), a tutor (paid, fast), certificate exams (basic, conversational, fluent; the fee and your odds in words; a certificate never fades and counts for jobs and for moving abroad), and the People interaction "Practice their language" with a native speaker you know (it raises skill and the tie, up to a yearly cap). A translation and interpreting job track needs two fluent languages. Parents can speak a language of their own at home with a child (a parenting choice that passes it on), heritage languages return in later-life events, and German joins the 22 languages. More → Plan a trip now has a kind of trip (vacation, cruise, honeymoon for a spouse you married in the last few years, a trip with friends, a business trip for jobs that call for travel), what you do (sightseeing, nightlife, adventure, food, language immersion) and a souvenir (a pricey one becomes one of the Luxury valuables); there are 10 new destinations (eight places and two cruises), and people met at places with a fitting name pool take their names and first language from it. Work/School → temporary stays can bring a partner and the children who live with you (they go to school where you live and come home with you). A retirement record now travels between the four countries: each country pays for its own years by its own formula (`balance/countries.yaml pensionPortability`; Money shows the rows). Numbers are in `balance/languages.yaml`, `balance/travel.yaml` and `balance/countries.yaml`; `npm run simulate` reports learning by method, exams, translators, bilingual children, trips by kind and activity, families on stays and pensions across countries, with targets in `balance/targets.yaml` (`languagesB10`, `travelB10`).

P1 adds a portrait for every character and person (docs/portraits-and-ui.md, `docs/design.md` → Portraits). The art is in `public/portraits` (about 190 MB, never precached by the service worker; credits in `CREDITS.md`) and the renderer in `src/portraits`. Everyone's look is saved settings (`look` on the character and each person); `balance/portraits.yaml` holds the age stages, graying ages, dye fade, smile threshold, generation odds, inheritance, hair and eye descriptor mapping and thumbnail cache sizes. Looks are rolled from a generator of their own (the life's seed plus the person's id), so they never move a life's random stream. Tools: `npx tsx tools/portraits/availability.ts` regenerates `src/portraits/options.ts` and `availability.ts` from the manifest (a test fails when they are stale); `python3 -I tools/portraits/patch_collared_shirt.py --src <pristine assets> --dest public/portraits` redoes the collared-shirt fix; `npx tsx tools/portraits/review-sheet.ts --before=<pristine assets>` then `python3 -I tools/portraits/join_sheet.py` rebuild the review sheets in `review/`; `npx vite build && npx tsx tools/portraits/measure.ts` measures first-portrait time and People-list scrolling in a real browser. The portrait package's own tests run with `npm test` (`tools/portraits`).

U1 redesigns the interface (docs/portraits-and-ui.md, `docs/design.md` → UI/UX Architecture). Home is the life log (grouped by age, newest at the bottom, money lines in green or red with the balance) under a header with your portrait, name, occupation, city and balance, with the Age button between four tabs (Job, which becomes School while you are enrolled, Assets, People, Activities) and the stat panel as bars with percentages. Dark is the default theme. The menu (☰) holds New life, Life history, Archive, Settings and Back to title; the profile holds Health and Later life; Assets holds Finances, Properties, Possessions and Your will; People holds Family planning, Groups and "Spend time with everyone" (`src/engine/interactions/group.ts`); Activities holds a "coming soon" card plus Leisure and self, Plan a trip, Move abroad, Social media and the teen years. Event cards show a category banner and border in the category's color (`registries/banners.yaml`, colors `--wl-cat-<id>` in `src/ui/theme/tokens.css`), an icon (`registries/icons.yaml`) or a person header, an optional `meter`, stacked choices with known costs, "Surprise me" (the choice id `surprise`, resolved with the life's seeded generator and logged like any choice) and outcomes with amounts and the new balance; events may set `banner`, `icon` and `meter`. `HistoryEntry.money` carries an outcome's amount and balance to the log. Content icons are Microsoft Fluent Emoji copied by `npm run icons -- <path to a fluentui-emoji checkout>` into `public/icons` (the content build fails if a listed icon's file or its credit in `CREDITS.md` is missing); interface icons are Lucide; type is Fredoka and Nunito through `@fontsource`. `tools/contrast.test.ts` checks every text and banner color pair in both themes against WCAG AA.
